## 2025-06-04 | CHANNEL ROI

**Employee Referral is the highest-converting channel but 
the most underfunded.**
237 applications → 5.5% app-to-join rate.
LinkedIn: 283 applications → 3.5% app-to-join rate.
Referral produces better candidates AND faster hires.
Most MNCs spend 3–5x more budget on LinkedIn than referrals.

→ *Implication:* Shifting even 20% of LinkedIn budget to a
referral incentive program (₹10,000–20,000 per successful hire)
would likely produce more hires at lower cost.
→ *Action:* Build a referral ROI model — cost per hire by channel.

## 2025-06-04 | SLA COMPLIANCE

**SLA4 (Offer Made → Accepted within 5 days) has the worst 
compliance rate in the dataset.**
Only 23 out of 70 candidates who received offers accepted 
within the SLA window (33% compliance).
SLA1 compliance: 55%. SLA2: 69%. SLA3: 100%. SLA4: 33%.

SLA3 at 100% looks suspicious — check if the column was 
populated for all interview records or only for completions.
SLA4 at 33% is the most damaging: slow offer acceptance 
correlates directly with offer decline and competing offers.

→ *Implication:* Candidates are sitting on offers for too long.
Recruiters are likely waiting for formal approval before 
communicating the offer, creating dead air the candidate fills 
by entertaining competing offers.
→ *Action:* Measure average days at Offer Made → Accepted stage.
Any candidate beyond day 3 needs a proactive touch.
→ *Links:* [[SLA Analysis]], [[Red Flags]]

## 2025-06-04 | OFFER DECLINE PATTERN

**Offer declines are evenly spread across all 6 reasons — 
no single dominant cause.**
Location Issues: 5 | Salary Too Low: 4 | Role Mismatch: 4 |
Accepted Competing Offer: 4 | Personal Reasons: 4 | 
Counter Offer: 2

Total offer declines: 23 candidates.
When no single reason dominates, the problem is systemic 
speed rather than a single fixable issue.
Candidates who decline for varied reasons are typically 
candidates who were never fully engaged — the process 
was too slow to keep them committed.

→ *Implication:* An offer declined for "Personal Reasons" at the
same rate as "Salary Too Low" suggests candidates are using 
neutral language to exit a process they've lost confidence in.
→ *Action:* Add post-decline survey or structured exit call.
→ *Links:* [[Methodology]], [[Channel Deep Dive]]

## 2025-06-04 | TIME TO FILL vs TIME TO HIRE

**Average Time to Fill: 51.8 days. Average Time to Hire: ~52 days.**
These are nearly identical — meaning requisitions are being 
opened at almost the same time candidates are applying.
In a well-run TA function, the req opens 1–2 weeks BEFORE 
active sourcing so JD approval, sourcing strategy, and 
channel activation happen before candidates enter.
A zero gap here suggests reactive hiring: reqs open only when 
a seat becomes urgently empty, not planned 2–3 weeks ahead.

→ *Implication:* HR is reacting to exits rather than anticipating 
workforce needs. This is a workforce planning problem, not a 
sourcing problem.
→ *Action:* Track average req-open-to-first-application time.
Anything under 7 days signals reactive hiring.
→ *Links:* [[Methodology]], [[SLA Analysis]]

## 2025-06-04 | ON HOLD PIPELINE RISK

**139 candidates are On Hold — 3.5x more than the total joined.**
This is the invisible pipeline problem. These candidates are
still "active" in the system, receiving no communication,
likely losing interest daily.
If 40% eventually receive an offer, that's 55 future candidates
who have been waiting in silence for weeks or months.

→ *Implication:* On Hold is often a euphemism for
"hiring manager can't decide" or "budget approval pending."
Both are fixable with process discipline.
→ *Action:* Every On Hold record needs a last-contacted date and 
a mandatory review trigger at 30 days.

## 2025-06-04 | CAMPUS RECRUITMENT UNDERPERFORMANCE

**Campus Recruitment: 90 applications, only 2 joined (2.2% conv.).**
Lowest conversion of any channel alongside Walk-in.
Campus typically has the longest TTH because it follows 
academic calendars — offer acceptance in November, 
joining in June creates a 6-month dead zone.
The low conversion here may reflect candidates accepting 
multiple campus offers and choosing a competitor at joining time.

→ *Implication:* Campus is a pipeline channel, not a fill channel.
It should not be used for urgent hiring needs.
→ *Action:* Separate campus TTH from non-campus TTH in reporting.
Build an engagement calendar for campus hires between 
offer acceptance and joining to reduce no-shows.
→ *Links:* [[Channel Deep Dive]]

## 2025-06-04 | SKILLS MISMATCH AS TOP REJECTION REASON

**Skills Mismatch is the #1 rejection reason in the dataset.**
This is a shortlisting quality problem, not a candidate quality problem.
If skills mismatch is being caught at the interview stage, 
it means screening is failing to filter candidates earlier.
Every skills-mismatch rejection at interview costs ~8–10 
business days of recruiter and hiring manager time that
should have been caught in the CV screen.

→ *Implication:* The job description or screening criteria are 
too broad, attracting candidates who look right on paper 
but cannot pass technical or competency evaluation.
→ *Action:* Add a must-have skills filter to the shortlisting 
checklist. Pilot a 15-minute pre-screen call before scheduling 
full panel interviews for technical roles.
→ *Links:* [[Red Flags]], [[Methodology]]

## 2025-06-04 | AGENCY CHANNEL ROI CONCERN

**Agency: 125 applications, 3 joined, 2.4% conversion.**
Agency fees are typically 15–18% of first-year CTC.
Average CTC Offered in the dataset: ~₹20–25 LPA range.
Estimated agency fee per hire: ₹3–4.5 lakhs.
Total agency spend for 3 hires: ~₹9–13.5 lakhs.
LinkedIn cost per hire via direct sourcing: significantly lower.

→ *Implication:* Agency is being used as a default channel rather 
than a specialist channel (for hard-to-fill or senior roles only).
→ *Action:* Restrict agency usage to roles open >45 days with
zero shortlists via direct channels. Track agency spend in the 
channel performance sheet.
